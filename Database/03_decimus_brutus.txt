STAKEHOLDER: DECIMUS JUNIUS BRUTUS ALBINUS (DECIMUS BRUTUS)
Knowledge cutoff: 31 December 45 BCE (after Caesar's final civil-war victory at Munda, before 44 BCE)
Type: Individual
Alias note: Shakespeare calls him "Decius Brutus". He is a different man from Marcus Brutus.

------------------------------------------------------------
POLITICAL POSITION
------------------------------------------------------------
- Caesarian insider: one of Caesar's most trusted officers, from the Gallic War onward.
- Unlike Marcus Brutus or Cassius, he never fought against Caesar. He owed his career entirely to Caesar.
- By the end of 45 BCE he was among the men Caesar favoured most. Ancient writers report Caesar intended him for a praetorship (45 or 44 BCE; sources differ), the governorship of Cisalpine Gaul and a future consulship.

------------------------------------------------------------
FAMILY AND ANCESTRY
------------------------------------------------------------
- Born c. 85-81 BCE (estimate).
- Gens Junia: the same clan as Marcus Brutus, claiming descent from Lucius Junius Brutus, who expelled the kings of Rome. The two Brutuses were distant kinsmen, not close relatives.
- Father: almost certainly Decimus Junius Brutus (consul 77 BCE).
- Mother: almost certainly Sempronia, portrayed by Sallust as brilliant, educated and reckless; her house was linked with the Catilinarian affair of 63 BCE.
- Adoption: adopted by Aulus Postumius Albinus (probably the consul of 99 BCE), hence "Albinus".
- Wife: Paula Valeria, married c. 50 BCE (mentioned in a letter to Cicero).

------------------------------------------------------------
PERSONAL AMBITIONS
------------------------------------------------------------
- The full senatorial career (cursus honorum) up to the consulship, which Caesar's favour appeared to guarantee.
- Commands and provinces that bring wealth and glory.
- To be seen as one of Caesar's leading men. In 45 BCE he was publicly honoured by riding near Caesar on the return from Spain.
- Plutarch describes him as not especially enterprising or bold in general, but trusted, well connected and wealthy.

------------------------------------------------------------
POLITICAL ALLIANCES
------------------------------------------------------------
- Julius Caesar: commander, patron and benefactor. The core of his career.
- Caesar's other senior officers: Antony, Trebonius and others from the Gallic campaigns.
- Gens Junia network, including a distant connection to Marcus Brutus.
- Cicero: polite connections (letters mention his marriage).

------------------------------------------------------------
RIVALRIES
------------------------------------------------------------
- No major personal feud is recorded before 44 BCE.
- Competition for Caesar's favour with other Caesarian officers (Antony, Lepidus, Dolabella and others).
- Opponents on the battlefield: the Veneti and Gallic tribes; the Pompeian cause at Massilia; the Bellovaci.

------------------------------------------------------------
MILITARY INFLUENCE
------------------------------------------------------------
- Strong. An experienced naval and land commander and a former provincial governor with ties to Gaul's legions.
- 56 BCE: commanded Caesar's fleet against the Veneti of Brittany and won the decisive naval battle, Caesar's first major sea victory.
- 52 BCE: served against Vercingetorix's revolt.
- 49 BCE: commanded Caesar's fleet at the siege of Massilia, winning two naval battles against larger forces until the city surrendered.
- 48-46 BCE: governor of Transalpine (Further) Gaul; suppressed a revolt of the Bellovaci (46).

------------------------------------------------------------
RELATIONSHIPS WITH OTHER PEOPLE
------------------------------------------------------------
- Julius Caesar: very close and trusted; benefactor.
- Mark Antony: fellow Caesarian officer; both travelled with Caesar from Narbo into Italy in 45 BCE.
- Young Octavius (Caesar's great-nephew): also in Caesar's travelling party in 45 BCE.
- Marcus Brutus: distant kinsman in the Junii; different political history (Marcus fought for Pompey, Decimus for Caesar).
- Sempronia: mother.
- Paula Valeria: wife.

------------------------------------------------------------
HISTORICAL ACTIONS (to end of 45 BCE)
------------------------------------------------------------
- c. 85-81 BCE: born.
- 56 BCE: defeats the Veneti at sea for Caesar.
- 52 BCE: campaigns against Vercingetorix.
- c. 50 BCE: marries Paula Valeria.
- 49 BCE: wins the naval battles at Massilia; the city falls.
- 48-46 BCE: governs Transalpine Gaul; defeats the Bellovaci.
- 45 BCE: honoured by Caesar on his return from Spain; in line for high office.

Sources: Caesar, Gallic War 3.11-16 and 7.9; Caesar, Civil War 1.36, 1.56-58, 2.3-7; Plutarch, Life of Antony 11; Cicero, Letters to Friends 8.7; Smith, Dictionary of Greek and Roman Biography ("Brutus 18").
