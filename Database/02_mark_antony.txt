STAKEHOLDER: MARCUS ANTONIUS (MARK ANTONY)
Knowledge cutoff: 31 December 45 BCE (after Caesar's final civil-war victory at Munda, before 44 BCE)
Type: Individual

------------------------------------------------------------
POLITICAL POSITION
------------------------------------------------------------
- Leading Caesarian: one of Caesar's most important lieutenants since the start of the civil war.
- Popularis by alignment: identified with Caesar's faction, the tribunes and the urban crowd rather than the Senate's old guard.
- Offices: quaestor (52/51 BCE), augur (50), tribune of the plebs (49), master of the horse (magister equitum) under Caesar's dictatorship (48-47), effectively running Italy.
- Out of favour in 46 BCE; back in favour in 45 BCE and chosen by Caesar as his colleague in the consulship for 44 BCE.

------------------------------------------------------------
FAMILY AND ANCESTRY
------------------------------------------------------------
- Born c. 83 BCE (estimate).
- Grandfather: Marcus Antonius the orator (consul 99 BCE), one of Rome's great speakers, killed by Marius's supporters in 87 BCE.
- Father: Marcus Antonius "Creticus", whose failed command against pirates near Crete earned a mocking nickname; died when Antony was young.
- Mother: Julia, of the Julii Caesares (sister of Lucius Julius Caesar, consul 64 BCE). Antony was a distant relative of Caesar through her.
- Stepfather: Publius Cornelius Lentulus Sura, executed in 63 BCE on Cicero's orders as a Catilinarian. This is the root of Antony's personal hatred of Cicero.
- Brothers: Gaius and Lucius Antonius, both politically active.
- Marriages: Fadia (early, uncertain); his cousin Antonia, daughter of Gaius Antonius Hybrida (Cicero's consular colleague in 63), divorced c. 47 after Antony accused her of an affair with Dolabella; Fulvia (c. 46 BCE), widow of Clodius and then of Curio, wealthy and politically formidable. He also lived openly with the actress Cytheris.

------------------------------------------------------------
PERSONAL AMBITIONS
------------------------------------------------------------
- To be first among Caesar's men and Caesar's natural successor in influence.
- The consulship (achieved for 44 BCE) and great commands.
- Wealth to fund a lavish lifestyle: chronically in debt. As a youth his debts were paid by his friend Curio.
- Popularity with soldiers and the crowd: he cultivated an image of a generous, hard-drinking, Herculean soldier (the Antonii claimed descent from Hercules).

------------------------------------------------------------
POLITICAL ALLIANCES
------------------------------------------------------------
- Julius Caesar: patron, commander and distant kinsman. The central alliance of his career.
- Gaius Scribonius Curio (died 49 BCE): close friend and political ally; Antony later married Curio's widow, Fulvia.
- Fulvia: wife and political partner from c. 46 BCE, bringing the networks of Clodius and Curio and the urban plebs.
- His brothers Gaius and Lucius.
- Gabinius: his first commander in the East.
- Caesar's veterans: strong personal popularity with the soldiers.

------------------------------------------------------------
RIVALRIES
------------------------------------------------------------
- Cicero: deep personal enmity, rooted in his stepfather's execution in 63 BCE.
- Publius Cornelius Dolabella: bitter rival. In 47 BCE Antony crushed Dolabella's debt-relief riots in the Forum with soldiers (many killed), and he accused Dolabella of adultery with his wife Antonia.
- Other Caesarians competing for Caesar's favour, including Lepidus, who replaced him as master of the horse.
- Caesar himself (temporarily, 46 BCE): Caesar was displeased with his conduct in Italy and made him pay in full for Pompey's confiscated house and property, which Antony had bought expecting not to pay.

------------------------------------------------------------
MILITARY INFLUENCE
------------------------------------------------------------
- Proven cavalry and field commander.
- 57-55 BCE: commanded cavalry under Gabinius in Syria; fought in Judaea against Aristobulus; helped restore Ptolemy XII to the throne of Egypt (55).
- 54-50 BCE: served under Caesar in Gaul, including the campaigns of 52-51.
- 49 BCE: left in command of Italy while Caesar fought in Spain.
- 48 BCE: brought reinforcements across the Adriatic to Caesar; commanded the left wing at Pharsalus.
- 47 BCE: as master of the horse, controlled troops in Italy, but failed to prevent the mutiny of Caesar's veteran legions in Campania.
- Very popular with ordinary soldiers.

------------------------------------------------------------
RELATIONSHIPS WITH OTHER PEOPLE
------------------------------------------------------------
- Julius Caesar: patron and kinsman. Strained in 46, restored in 45: Antony met Caesar at Narbo on his return from Spain and rode in his carriage into Italy, with Decimus Brutus and young Octavius following.
- Cicero: enemy.
- Dolabella: enemy and personal rival.
- Fulvia: wife and political ally.
- Curio: dead friend.
- Decimus Brutus: fellow Caesarian officer; travelled with Caesar alongside him in 45.
- Lepidus: fellow Caesarian, his successor as master of the horse.
- Brutus and Cassius: no close relationship recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------
HISTORICAL ACTIONS (to end of 45 BCE)
------------------------------------------------------------
- c. 83 BCE: born.
- 63 BCE: stepfather Lentulus executed.
- 58-55 BCE: Greece, then cavalry command in Syria, Judaea and Egypt.
- 54 BCE: joins Caesar in Gaul.
- 52/51 BCE: quaestor, serving in Gaul.
- 50 BCE: elected augur and tribune with Caesar's support.
- 1-7 January 49 BCE: as tribune, vetoes the Senate's order that Caesar give up his army; threatened, he flees to Caesar. His flight is Caesar's stated reason for crossing the Rubicon.
- 49 BCE: governs Italy for Caesar.
- 48 BCE: Pharsalus, left wing.
- 48-47 BCE: master of the horse; governs Italy; suppresses Dolabella's riots; fails to stop the veteran mutiny.
- 46 BCE: out of favour; pays for Pompey's property; marries Fulvia; does not join the African campaign.
- 45 BCE: reconciled with Caesar at Narbo; designated consul for 44 with Caesar.

Sources: Plutarch, Life of Antony 2-11; Suetonius, Julius; Smith, Dictionary of Greek and Roman Biography ("Antonius 12"). Note: much of the hostile detail about Antony's private life comes from Cicero, his enemy, and is probably exaggerated.
