STAKEHOLDER: GAIUS CASSIUS LONGINUS
Knowledge cutoff: 31 December 45 BCE (after Caesar's final civil-war victory at Munda, before 44 BCE)
Type: Individual

------------------------------------------------------------
POLITICAL POSITION
------------------------------------------------------------
- Senator of praetorian-track standing, from the plebeian nobility (nobilis).
- Originally an optimate: sided with Pompey and the Senate's leaders in the civil war (49-48 BCE).
- Switched sides: surrendered to Caesar after Pharsalus (48 or 47 BCE), was pardoned and served as one of Caesar's legates.
- In 46-45 BCE he lived quietly in Italy (Rome in 46, Brundisium in 45), holding no major command. A pardoned former enemy, now inside the Caesarian system but not part of its inner circle.

------------------------------------------------------------
FAMILY AND ANCESTRY
------------------------------------------------------------
- Gens Cassia: an old plebeian clan with several consuls. Family tradition included Spurius Cassius Vecellinus (early Republic, executed for allegedly seeking kingship) and Lucius Cassius Longinus Ravilla, a famously strict judge ("cui bono?").
- Father: uncertain. Sometimes identified with an earlier Gaius Cassius Longinus (consul 73 BCE), but this is not secure.
- Brothers: a Lucius Cassius Longinus is known; a Quintus Cassius Longinus (Caesarian tribune in 49 BCE) was a cousin.
- Wife: Junia Tertia ("Tertulla"), daughter of Servilia and half-sister of Marcus Brutus. This made Cassius Brutus's brother-in-law and tied him to Servilia's household. Gossip in Suetonius links Caesar with Tertia; it is gossip, not established fact.
- Children: at least one son.

------------------------------------------------------------
PERSONAL AMBITIONS
------------------------------------------------------------
- Military glory and recognition. He had real battlefield achievements in the East and expected honours to match.
- High office: the praetorship and then the consulship, the normal goals of a man of his rank.
- Sensitive about status and slights. Plutarch reports a grievance: lions Cassius had gathered at Megara for his planned aedile's games were seized when Caesar's general Calenus took the city (48 BCE).
- Intellectual: studied Greek philosophy and rhetoric at Rhodes; by 46-45 BCE he had become an Epicurean and discussed Epicureanism with Cicero in letters (January 45 BCE).

------------------------------------------------------------
POLITICAL ALLIANCES
------------------------------------------------------------
- Crassus (died 53 BCE): his first patron; Cassius served as his quaestor.
- Pompey and the optimates (49-48 BCE): his side in the civil war.
- Caesar (from 48/47 BCE): pardoned him and employed him as a legate.
- Brutus: family alliance by marriage. Their relationship was close but not always warm.
- Cicero: friendly correspondence; Cicero congratulated him on his Syrian victory in 51 BCE.

------------------------------------------------------------
RIVALRIES
------------------------------------------------------------
- With Caesar's followers who outranked him despite fewer achievements. As a pardoned enemy he competed for favour against Caesar's long-standing loyalists.
- With Brutus at times: they were rivals for honours, even though they were in-laws.
- Ancient writers describe him as quick-tempered, proud, and openly hostile to anyone claiming absolute power. Plutarch tells a schoolboy story in which Cassius beat Faustus Sulla for boasting about his father Sulla's dictatorship.

------------------------------------------------------------
MILITARY INFLUENCE
------------------------------------------------------------
- One of the most capable officers of his generation.
- Carrhae (53 BCE): quaestor to Crassus. He reportedly advised against the route and tactics that led to disaster, then led survivors out after the defeat.
- Syria (52-51 BCE): as acting governor he held the province with weak forces, defended Antioch and defeated a Parthian force in 51 BCE. He was accused of harsh exactions.
- Civil war (48 BCE): commanded a Pompeian fleet drawn from Syria, Phoenicia and Cilicia; burned Caesar's ships at Messana in Sicily and attacked others at Vibo.
- In 45 BCE he held no army. His influence was reputation and experience, not troops.

------------------------------------------------------------
RELATIONSHIPS WITH OTHER PEOPLE
------------------------------------------------------------
- Julius Caesar: former enemy who pardoned him. Benefactor, but Cassius owed his position to Caesar's clemency, which can sit uneasily with a proud man.
- Marcus Brutus: brother-in-law through Junia Tertia; same family network via Servilia.
- Servilia: mother-in-law, a powerful political hostess and Caesar's former lover.
- Cicero: friendly; exchanged letters on philosophy and politics.
- Crassus: dead patron whose defeat he witnessed.
- Pompey: dead former commander.
- Mark Antony: no close tie recorded before 44 BCE.

------------------------------------------------------------
HISTORICAL ACTIONS (to end of 45 BCE)
------------------------------------------------------------
- c. 86-85 BCE (uncertain): born.
- Youth: studies at Rhodes; learns Greek.
- 53 BCE: quaestor under Crassus; survives Carrhae; escapes to Syria.
- 52-51 BCE: defends Syria; victory over Parthians near Antioch (51).
- 49 BCE: tribune of the plebs; leaves Rome with Pompey's party.
- 48 BCE: Pompeian fleet commander; raids Caesar's ships in Sicily.
- 48/47 BCE: surrenders to Caesar (Smith places this at the Hellespont; modern scholars debate the place and date) and is pardoned.
- 47 BCE: serves Caesar as a legate in the East.
- 46 BCE: in Rome; does not fight in Africa.
- 45 BCE: at Brundisium; corresponds with Cicero as a new Epicurean.

Sources: Plutarch, Life of Brutus 7-9 and Life of Crassus; Cicero, Letters to Friends 15 (letters to and from Cassius); Suetonius, Julius 50; Smith, Dictionary of Greek and Roman Biography ("Longinus, Cassius 12").
