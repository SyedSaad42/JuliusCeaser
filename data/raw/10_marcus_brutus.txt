STAKEHOLDER: MARCUS JUNIUS BRUTUS
Knowledge cutoff: 31 December 45 BCE (after Caesar's final civil-war victory at Munda, before 44 BCE)
Type: Individual
Alias note: also styled Quintus Servilius Caepio Brutus after his adoption by his uncle. Do not confuse him with Decimus Brutus.

------------------------------------------------------------
POLITICAL POSITION
------------------------------------------------------------
- Senator with a strong optimate background and philosophical convictions about liberty and virtue.
- Fought for Pompey in the civil war (49-48 BCE), even though Pompey had caused his father's death, because he judged Pompey's cause the better one for the state.
- Pardoned by Caesar immediately after Pharsalus (48 BCE) and treated as an honoured companion. Caesar made him governor of Cisalpine Gaul (46-45 BCE), where his honest rule contrasted with the plundering of other governors.
- By late 45 BCE he was one of the most respected men in Rome: favoured by Caesar, admired by Cicero and the former Pompeians, and the nephew and son-in-law of Cato. Both camps regarded him as theirs.
- In August 45 BCE, after meeting Caesar returning from Spain, he told Cicero that Caesar was "going over to the good men" (the optimates). Cicero doubted it.

------------------------------------------------------------
FAMILY AND ANCESTRY
------------------------------------------------------------
- Born c. 85 BCE.
- Father's line: the Junii Bruti, claiming descent from Lucius Junius Brutus, who expelled the last king of Rome, Tarquin, and founded the Republic. Hostile writers disputed this descent.
- Mother's line: Servilia's family, the Servilii, claimed descent from Gaius Servilius Ahala, who killed Spurius Maelius when Maelius was accused of seeking absolute power. Both sides of his ancestry were famous enemies of kings.
- Father: Marcus Junius Brutus (tribune 83 BCE), killed in 77 BCE on Pompey's orders after surrendering during the revolt of Lepidus. Brutus long refused to speak to Pompey because of this.
- Mother: Servilia, half-sister of Cato the Younger, a powerful political figure and for years Caesar's lover. Caesar gave her a pearl said to be worth six million sesterces. Rumour said Caesar thought Brutus might be his son; Caesar's age (about 15 in 85 BCE) makes this very unlikely.
- Uncle and later father-in-law: Cato the Younger, whom Brutus admired above all Romans. Cato killed himself at Utica in 46 BCE rather than accept Caesar's pardon.
- Adoption: adopted by his maternal uncle Quintus Servilius Caepio, hence the name Caepio Brutus.
- Half-sisters: three Juniae; Junia Tertia married Cassius, and another Junia married Lepidus.
- First wife: Claudia, daughter of Appius Claudius Pulcher; divorced in 45 BCE.
- Second wife: Porcia, Cato's daughter and widow of Bibulus (Caesar's old enemy); married in mid-45 BCE. Servilia reportedly disliked the match, and Cicero noted the gossip.

------------------------------------------------------------
PERSONAL AMBITIONS
------------------------------------------------------------
- Moral reputation: to be, and to be seen as, a man of virtue in the mould of Cato and Plato's philosophers.
- Philosophy and writing: follower of the Old Academy of Antiochus of Ascalon; wrote on virtue (De Virtute), wrote a eulogy of Cato (46-45 BCE), made an abridgement of Polybius, and wrote in a terse, Spartan Greek style.
- A traditional career up to the consulship, achieved honourably.
- Wealth: despite his ideals, he lent money at high interest. Cicero, as governor of Cilicia (51-50 BCE), discovered that Brutus's agents were lending to the city of Salamis in Cyprus at 48% a year (four times the legal limit) and pressing for collection with force. Cicero refused to enforce it. This is a clear gap between his stated values and his conduct.
- Caesar's verdict, after hearing him speak (47 BCE): "I do not know what this young man wants, but whatever he wants, he wants very much."

------------------------------------------------------------
POLITICAL ALLIANCES
------------------------------------------------------------
- Cato: uncle, model and father-in-law.
- Cicero: close friend and literary partner; Cicero named his history of oratory Brutus (46 BCE) after him and dedicated Orator, On Ends and the Tusculan Disputations to him.
- Pompey (died 48 BCE): his side in the civil war despite the family grievance.
- Caesar (from 48 BCE): pardoner, patron and his mother's lover; gave him Cisalpine Gaul.
- Cassius: brother-in-law; Brutus pleaded with Caesar for Cassius's pardon.
- Appius Claudius Pulcher: his first father-in-law; Brutus served as his quaestor in Cilicia (53 BCE).
- The former Pompeian network, which saw him as its most respected figure.

------------------------------------------------------------
RIVALRIES
------------------------------------------------------------
- Pompey (before 49 BCE): personal enmity over his father's death. In 52 BCE Brutus wrote a pamphlet attacking the idea of a dictatorship for Pompey.
- Caesar's grasping followers: he despised flatterers and men who could not refuse a favour.
- Occasional friction with Cassius, despite their family tie.
- No open hostility toward Caesar is recorded before the cutoff.

------------------------------------------------------------
MILITARY INFLUENCE
------------------------------------------------------------
- Limited. He was not a career soldier.
- Served as a legate with Sestius in Cilicia (49 BCE), then joined Pompey in Macedonia and fought at Pharsalus (48 BCE). Caesar ordered his officers to spare him.
- As governor of Cisalpine Gaul (46-45 BCE) he controlled the province's forces, but his reputation there was administrative, not military.
- His real influence was moral authority and the name of Brutus.

------------------------------------------------------------
RELATIONSHIPS WITH OTHER PEOPLE
------------------------------------------------------------
- Julius Caesar: pardoner and benefactor, his mother's long-time lover and the rumoured (but unlikely) father. Trusted adviser after Pharsalus: Brutus correctly guessed that Pompey had fled to Egypt. They met again in 45 BCE on Caesar's return from Spain, on good terms.
- Servilia: mother; strong influence; Caesar's lover.
- Cato: uncle and moral model; died 46 BCE.
- Porcia: wife from 45 BCE; Cato's daughter, known for her devotion and strong will.
- Cassius: brother-in-law (married to his half-sister Junia Tertia).
- Cicero: close friend and admirer.
- Lepidus: brother-in-law (married to another half-sister).
- Pompey: his father's killer, later his commander.
- Decimus Brutus: distant kinsman in the Junii.
- His household philosophers and rhetoricians: Aristus (brother of Antiochus) and Empylus.

------------------------------------------------------------
HISTORICAL ACTIONS (to end of 45 BCE)
------------------------------------------------------------
- c. 85 BCE: born.
- 77 BCE: father killed on Pompey's orders.
- 58-56 BCE: accompanies Cato to Cyprus; converts King Ptolemy of Cyprus's treasure into money for Rome; wins Cato's praise.
- c. 55-54 BCE: as moneyer, issues coins showing Libertas and his ancestors Lucius Brutus and Servilius Ahala.
- 54 BCE: marries Claudia.
- 53 BCE: quaestor in Cilicia under Appius Claudius.
- 52 BCE: writes against a dictatorship for Pompey.
- 51-50 BCE: his high-interest loan to Salamis is exposed by Cicero.
- 49 BCE: joins Pompey; legate in Cilicia; then Macedonia.
- 48 BCE: fights at Pharsalus; escapes to Larissa; writes to Caesar; pardoned; advises Caesar that Pompey has fled to Egypt.
- 47 BCE: pleads for Cassius's pardon; speaks for a client king (Plutarch's text says "the king of Africa"; usually identified as Deiotarus of Galatia) at Nicaea.
- 46-45 BCE: governs Cisalpine Gaul for Caesar; honours from the province.
- 46-45 BCE: writes a eulogy of Cato; Cicero dedicates several works to him.
- 45 BCE: divorces Claudia; marries Porcia; meets Caesar returning from Spain and reports favourably on him to Cicero.

Sources: Plutarch, Life of Brutus 1-6 (your file, pre-cutoff chapters only); Cicero, Letters to Atticus 5.21, 6.1-3 (the Salamis loan), 13.9-10, 13.40 (Brutus on Caesar, August 45) and the dialogue Brutus; Suetonius, Julius 50 (Servilia); Plutarch, Life of Cato the Younger.
